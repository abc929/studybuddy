“I couldn’t imagine the day being more perfect to lay Herr Engel to rest,” Elsie said, looking up at the sparkling blue sky and the puffy clouds, pure white and edged with bright light. Rays of sunshine streaked through the sky. Fritz watched Peter and Jakob shovel the last of the dirt on the grave, which lay right next to the other four graves that had lain there for thirty-seven years. “I can’t even express how much I will miss Herr Engel. I can’t believe the infection took him so fast.” “He was so sick,” said Elsie. “I still can’t believe he wrote us each an individual farewell letter. Up to the very last, he was always thinking of others. Oh, I—I’m sorry, Fritz.” “It’s OK, Elsie. I don’t know why he didn’t write me a letter. Maybe he was saving mine for last and just got too sick. Oh, I feel I can hardly stand him being gone. At the same time, I feel peaceful, almost happy, thinking of him being at rest, having lived such a good life.” “I feel the same way. Look! Everyone is starting to arrive for the service. Are you nervous?” “Not really; it’s not that hard to share how I feel about Herr Engel. I just hope everyone doesn’t think it is strange that I, a fifteen-year-old boy, am going to speak instead of a preacher.” “Everyone knows there is no preacher around anywhere and that Herr Engel asked for you to speak.” Under the smiling sky, Fritz looked around before he began speaking. He had hardly been able to look at his mother the past few days without tearing up because he was so grateful for her sacrifice in buying Chocolate. He quickly smiled at each of his sisters. Farmer Becker’s family was there in their best clothes, including Günter. Several other families from neighboring farms were also there. A hymn was sung, and then Fritz cleared his throat and began. “It wasn’t long ago that I wanted to be someone important—a great man. I wanted to do something remarkable in this world—something that would really make a difference. I thought of all the famous people in the world, and I longed to be like them. But I was wrong. Herr Engel has shown me what it really means to be a great person. Herr Engel is one of the greatest people this world has known.” Fritz paused for a few seconds as he struggled to keep back tears. “What makes a person great? Writing a musical masterpiece? Creating a sculpture displayed in a museum? Having wealth? These can be good things, but a person can do these things and be the least great of all people, for what makes a person truly great is measured by how a person loves and follows our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. He is the way, and Herr Engel knew that. The teachings of Jesus Christ lived in him, shone in his face, filled his thoughts, formed his words, and moved his heart and hands. Today, as we lay to rest a truly great and important man, we are reminded that this life is temporary. It will not be many years until each of us is also laid to rest. So the question is, what do you want said of you in the end? What kind of person do you want to be remembered as? For me, I want to be known as a person who had the hope and light of Jesus Christ shining in my face, filling my thoughts, forming my words, and moving my heart and hands—just like Herr Engel. Today, we lay to rest a father, a husband, a friend, and greatest of all, a devoted disciple of Jesus Christ.” As Fritz finished, Günter had the only dry eyes in the group. He hung his head and quietly stood as the group talked amongst themselves at the end of the service. He looked up, surprised, when Fritz put his hand on his shoulder. “My good man, Günter, I’m glad you came.” “Don’t call me good, and I had no choice about coming.” “I’m glad you’re here anyway. I have something for you.” Fritz held out a book to Günter. “It’s Herr Engel’s Bible. He gave it to me after my accident.” Günter looked at Fritz and shook his head slightly. “No . . . I don’t want it. I didn’t even know Herr Engel.” “Exactly! That’s why I want you to have it. It has his markings and notes. I was blessed to know Herr Engel, and I want you to come to know him too, through reading his Bible. Apparently, Herr Engel has no living family that we could find, so my father said I could have the Bible that belonged to Herr Engel’s wife. I don’t need this one anymore.” “No, I don’t read the Bible, and why would you give it to me? I am not—you should give it to someone more important.” Günter looked uncomfortable. “I don’t think you understand just how important you are, Günter. But I want you to understand. Don’t you want to be happy?” Günter lifted his chin slightly and looked away. “I’m plenty happy.” “Please take it, Günter. Will you just try reading it? This book brings the comfort and hope that I want you to have.” Günter took the book. “You are the most interesting boy I’ve ever met, Fritz. You’re kind of crazy. Maybe I’ll read it. Maybe I won’t. But I’m just borrowing it—that’s all.”
