“Our father grew up on a farm in the Black Forest,” Lisette started. “He became a successful lawyer and moved to the city where he met our mother, who was born in Ireland. Our mother died when Heidi was born. A couple of years ago, we visited Father’s cousin in the Black Forest. He lives near here, and he told us about the nun’s story, so we wanted to visit the abbey. The stories they say about this place are not true, so we weren’t afraid to explore the abbey’s forest. We came across this woodcutter’s hut, which was abandoned, but we’ll get back to the hut later on. “While visiting Father’s cousin, Garit fell in love with the land in the Black Forest and wanted so much to be a farmer, but, of course, we didn’t have a farm. Then, my father’s brother, his only sibling, died. He was a wealthy man who never married. Father inherited a lot of money from him. One day, a little over a year ago, Father gathered us in his office.” As Lisette unfolded the story, it seemed to come alive in Fritz’s mind. “Children,” said our father, his eyes filled with excitement. “Your uncle has left us a lot of money, enough to buy a farm in the Black Forest.” Garit leaned forward. “Oh, Father, are you really thinking we would move to the Black Forest?” “Yes, I am. Being a lawyer is wearing on me. I long for the peace of the green hills, and I know that you do too, Garit. We can have a big, new farmhouse built on a beautiful piece of land. What do you think, girls?” Heidi danced around, and I clapped my hands. “When will we leave, Father?” Garit asked. “Right away. I have a buyer for our house, and we are leaving all the furniture.” “But the farmhouse is not built yet,” I stated. “If we are leaving right away, where will we go?” “Here’s my plan,” said Father. “Do you remember that abandoned woodcutter’s hut by that old abbey?” We all nodded our heads. We happily remembered the clear, gurgling stream next to the hut and the beautiful forest that surrounded it. “We can stay there temporarily and start building the farmhouse as soon as we find the perfect piece of land. We might be in the woodcutter’s hut for a year, but we can fix it up nicely, and it will be cozy. What do you think?” The plan was readily agreed upon, and within two weeks our happy group was off in our own little wagon, carrying the few possessions we had decided to bring with us. One of the first things Father did was hide the large sum of money that he had brought to purchase the land and the house, intending to put it in the bank soon.” Lisette stopped the story to dish up steaming bowls of stew for all of them. Fritz was polite enough to let Lisette eat her late lunch before continuing her story, which she did as soon as the bowls were cleared. She started with a sigh. “But Father died unexpectedly in his sleep, just a couple days after we arrived here. He had complained that his chest hurt before going to bed. We believe his heart failed him.” Fritz could not help the tears that sprang to his eyes, and Lisette saw them. “It was hard, Fritz, but Father was such a good man. We know he is with Mother now, and resting with God. His motto was always ‘be of good cheer!’ He told us that almost every day of our lives.” Fritz’s heart swelled, and he pulled out the wooden disc from his pocket that said, “Be of Good Cheer.” He showed it to Lisette. “This is from a dear friend of mine who also passed away. This is my motto, too.” “Oh, Fritz!” cried Lisette. “How marvelous! Well, we tried to be of good cheer, but it wasn’t always easy. We did not know where Father had hidden the money, and we had only enough food to last us for a couple of weeks. So Garit went to work in the mines, and we stayed here to search for Father’s money. The mines are not close enough for Garit to come home at night, so he made a door in that tree you saw. It is partway between here and the mines. There he would leave the money he earned each week, and we would leave him notes to let him know how we were doing.” “Ah!” exclaimed Fritz. “That makes sense! The mystery is solved! Oh, but I’m so sorry that I discovered you. You said you could not use the door in the tree after that. Of course, you were worried that your money and notes would be found.” “Yes, and we didn’t want anyone to know that we lived in the woodcutter’s hut. We were worried that we might be taken to an orphanage or separated from each other. We didn’t think it would take us so long to find the money,” Lisette explained. “We found another tree for our messages, and we kept looking for the money, digging a lot, thinking maybe he buried it.” “But we found it!” chimed in Heidi. “Where was it?” asked Fritz at the edge of his seat. “In our clock!” “Your clock?” cried Fritz. “Yes,” replied Heidi. “Father bought it on the way here, saying that every Black Forest family needed a cuckoo clock. It was rather large for our little hut, but it made it feel like we really were a Black Forest family.” Lisette resumed the story. “A few days ago, I accidentally knocked the clock off the wall with the broomstick while I was cleaning cobwebs. It broke open, and the money spilled out. We immediately left a message for Garit, and he got home just before you arrived. I was just returning from town after buying some food when I came across you.” “Incredible!” exclaimed Fritz. “Absolutely incredible! What will you do with the money now that you have found it?” “Buy a farm, of course,” said Garit. “We are going to start looking right away!” “What about you?” asked Lisette. “Will your family be worried about you? We have a couple of horses outside. Maybe you and Garit could ride to your home?” “Oh, that would be wonderful.” “Does he need to leave right away?” asked Heidi. “It’s been so long since we talked to anyone. Can’t Fritz stay a little while and tell us about his family?” “I absolutely can!” declared Fritz. “My family is out of town and won’t arrive home until tomorrow morning. I’m pretty sure Chocolate, my horse, made his way home and is grazing in our field by now. If you want to hear my story, I can tell it to you. I actually wrote it down, but let me start from the beginning. One thing first—will you all come over for lunch tomorrow and meet my family?” “We would love to!” said Lisette with a huge smile. “We are so very glad we met you, Fritz!”
